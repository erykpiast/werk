## What to build

`werk target [<path>]` sets or prints the project's target repository; it refuses a path that is not a directory, has no main line, or lies inside or holds werk's store. With no target, a build stage exits 1 and names the target operation.

`werk plan <subtree-id>` reads the scope in two tiers. Tier one is the subtree and every card a subtree card links by depends_on or by a `#id` in its title, why or body. Tier two is everything else reached by following each scope card's depends_on targets with their subtrees, until nothing new comes in, never from a card to its parent. Only decided cards enter; the walk stops at open, proto, stale or postponed cards. An open or proto card in tier one stops the plan before any agent runs.

The plan runs the spec writer, then the ticket writer, each a fresh agent with its role in the environment, the werk instructions and the build-rule titles, the tiers apart in its prompt, read and write tools only, and clamped time limits. The spec goes to werk's store per subtree; tickets to `<subtree-id>/NN`. Each writer also writes a list of answers that no card gives; the plan proposes each as a ghost under the subtree root with who me and the id `<root>-<slug>`, and reports and exits with an error for a record it cannot propose. The plan runs the ticket checks: a ticket that fails its own checks is not planned; the rest go on the board; a failed blocked_by check puts the named tickets in blocked with the reason; the plan exits 1 on any set failure. Every new ticket starts in ready or blocked. Plan started, each step with its agent log, and plan ended go to the board log.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] With scripted spec and ticket writers, a plan writes a spec, puts valid tickets on the board, and exits 0.
- [ ] A tier-one open card stops the plan before any agent launch.
- [ ] A tier-two card reached through a depends_on chain appears in the writers' prompts; a sibling under its parent does not.
- [ ] Each writer answer becomes a ghost under the subtree root, and an already-taken id fails the plan with a message.
- [ ] A ticket that fails its own checks is not on the board.
- [ ] Each writer launch carries its role, and the plan's start, steps and end are in the board log.
